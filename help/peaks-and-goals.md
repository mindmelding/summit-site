---
title: Climb a peak
description: Pick a mountain, bank every walk's climb toward it, and see when you'll reach the top.
---

Your goal is one mountain at a time. Every walk you save adds its climb, until you've climbed the mountain's full height from sea level.

## Pick a peak

Open the **Goal** tab. Summit has 111 peaks in groups:

| Group | Peaks | For example |
|---|---|---|
| San Diego Warm-ups | 8 | Cowles Mountain, 1,594 ft |
| Seven Summits | 8 | Kilimanjaro, Aconcagua, Everest |
| 8000 m Giants | 13 | K2, Lhotse |
| North America | 26 | Mount Whitney, Mount Logan |
| South America | 9 | Chimborazo |
| Europe | 21 | Mont Blanc |
| Africa & Middle East | 6 | Mount Kenya |
| Asia | 17 | Ama Dablam |
| Oceania & Antarctica | 3 | Aoraki / Mount Cook |

Search by name or country at the top of the list. Tap a peak to see its outline and its route up, then tap **Climb** and the peak's name to make it your goal.

:::tip
Start small. A San Diego warm-up takes a handful of walks. Everest at 300 ft a walk takes about a hundred.
:::

## Your peak page

Once you've picked a peak, the Goal tab shows:

- How far up you are, as a percentage and on the mountain's outline.
- **To go**, **Next** (the next milestone on the way up) and **Last walk**.
- How often you walk, such as **2 walks a week**.
- After two walks, the date you'll reach the top at your pace over the last four weeks.

Some peaks note the tree line and the snow line. Climb past them and the page cools to match.

## Reach the top

When your banked climb reaches the peak's height, the page is stamped **SUMMITED**. Tap **Add to summit shelf** to keep it. Your shelf sits at the top of the peak list, with the date and the number of walks each one took. Then pick your next peak.

## Change your peak

Tap **⋯** on your peak page, then **Change peak**. If you've already climbed some of the current peak, Summit asks what to do with it:

- **Carry it over** moves every walk logged for the old peak to the new one.
- **Start fresh** keeps those walks in your log, but they stop counting toward any peak.
