title: Privacy
description: What Summit keeps on your phone, and the little it sends elsewhere.
---

Your walks, paths and peak stay on your iPhone. There's no account and no Summit server.

## What stays on your phone

- Your walks: the path, climb, distance and time.
- Your peak, your progress and your summit shelf.
- Your settings.

Deleting the app deletes all of it.

## What leaves your phone

- **Route planning** sends your start point, and your end point if you set one, to GraphHopper, the route service. Nothing else goes with them.
- **Place search** sends what you type to Apple Maps.

- **Usage and crash reports** go to PostHog, the analytics service Summit uses. They say when the app opens, when a route search finds options, and when a walk is saved, with that walk's climb, distance and time. They also carry crash reports so we can fix bugs. They never include your location, your path or your name. Each install gets a random ID that isn't tied to you or your Apple ID.

Tracking a walk works in airplane mode. The walk-saved report waits until you're back online.

## What Summit asks for

| Permission | Why |
|---|---|
| Location, while using the app | To record your path and distance, and to start plans where you stand |
| Motion & Fitness | To read the barometer for your climb |
| Notifications | To tell you at halfway and at your target. Summit doesn't ask; they arrive quietly until you turn on alerts |
| Add to Photos | Only when you tap **Save to Photos** on a walk card |

Summit doesn't sell data, show ads, or track you across other apps and websites.
